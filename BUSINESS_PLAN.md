# Meridian — Business Plan

## The problem

High-income professionals are financially complex but underserved.

A senior engineer at a tech company might have: a 401(k), a Roth IRA, a taxable brokerage, RSUs vesting on a schedule, an ESPP, a mortgage, a 529 for their kid, and a life insurance policy they haven't reviewed in four years. They earn enough to have real financial decisions to make — tax optimization, asset location, when to exercise options, whether to buy vs. rent — but those decisions require synthesizing information across every account they own.

Their options today:
- **DIY**: use spreadsheets, Personal Capital, and Reddit. Time-consuming, easy to miss things, no coherent plan.
- **Hire a financial advisor**: $5,000–$15,000/year for a good one. Meetings once or twice a year. The advisor doesn't have live access to your data and can't answer questions on Tuesday at 9pm.
- **Robo-advisors**: handle one account, one dimension. Don't touch tax, estate, cash flow, or planning.

None of these give you a live, whole-picture view of your finances with specific, personalized next steps.

---

## Who Meridian is for

**Primary:** High-income W-2 earners with financial complexity — tech workers, doctors, lawyers, consultants, senior executives. Household income $200k+. They have multiple accounts, equity compensation, and real tax exposure. They can afford a financial advisor but find the relationship too slow and too infrequent to be useful day-to-day.

**Secondary:** People at financial inflection points — first job with RSUs, getting married, having a child, buying a home, approaching retirement. They have a specific question that requires a real answer, not generic advice.

**Not for (yet):** People with very simple finances (one income, one account, no investments). The value is in synthesizing complexity — without complexity, there's nothing to synthesize.

---

## What makes Meridian different

### 1. Whole-picture, always current

Most tools own one slice: Mint tracks spending, Vanguard tracks your Vanguard accounts, TurboTax handles taxes once a year. Meridian connects everything and holds it together. Every time you open the app, you see your full financial picture updated to today — not a snapshot from last quarter.

### 2. Findings, not scores

Meridian doesn't tell you your "financial health score is 72." It tells you: your bond allocation is in your taxable account and it should be in your 401(k) instead — fixing that would save you an estimated $1,800/year in taxes. Specific. Estimated impact. Actionable.

### 3. A planner you can talk to

Every screen has a chat interface with full context — the agent knows your income, your accounts, your goals, and what it just showed you. You can ask "why is my estimated tax so high?" and get an answer that cites your specific numbers. You can ask "what happens if I exercise my options in December instead of January?" and get a real projection.

### 4. Scenario simulation via Briefs

When you have a big decision — retire early, sell your house, take a new job with a pay cut — Meridian lets you simulate it. A Brief is a full analysis of the scenario: the headline outcome, the assumptions the agent made, the reasoning, and the levers you can pull to explore alternatives. It's not generic advice; it's your numbers, your situation, your tradeoffs.

### 5. No action, no conflict of interest

Meridian does not manage your money, sell you products, or take a percentage of AUM. It is purely an analysis and planning layer. Its only incentive is to give you accurate information.

## The seven domains Meridian covers

| Domain | What it tracks | Example finding |
|--------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Overview** | Goal progress bars (retirement, home, college, etc.), net worth trajectory, savings rate | "At your current pace, you'll reach your retirement target 3 years late" |
| **Cash Flow** | Income vs. spending, account structure, liquidity buffer | "Your savings is split across 4 accounts with no clear purpose per account; you have 2.1 months of expenses in cash, below your 3-month target" |
| **Tax** | Current-year liability, asset location, year-end opportunities, cross-border situations | "You have $12k of unrealized losses to harvest before year-end; your bond funds are in taxable accounts and should move to your 401(k)" |
| **Equity & Compensation** | RSU vesting strategy, option exercise timing, ESPP participation, employer stock concentration | "32% of your net worth is in employer stock from unvested RSUs and holdings — above the recommended 10% threshold" |
| **Retirement & Benefits** | 401(k)/IRA/Roth/HSA contributions, backdoor Roth eligibility, employer benefits elections | "Your employer offers an HSA-eligible plan you haven't switched to — estimated annual tax savings: $2,200; mega backdoor Roth is available through your 401(k) and unused" |
| **Investments** | Allocation vs. target, fund fees, tax-efficient placement, drift and rebalancing | "Your international allocation has drifted 6% above target; switching to lower-cost index equivalents would save $800/year in fees" |
| **Risk & Estate** | Life/disability insurance coverage, estate document status, beneficiary designations, dependent coverage gaps | "Your life insurance beneficiary is outdated; your will predates the birth of your child" |

## Real use case walkthrough: Priya, 38, Staff Engineer at a tech company

**Situation:** Priya earns $280k/year (salary + bonus). She has $180k in RSUs vesting this year, a 401(k) with $310k, a taxable brokerage with $95k, and a mortgage. She and her husband have a 4-year-old. She's thinking about retiring at 55 but has no idea if it's realistic. She pays a lot in taxes and suspects she's not being smart about it but doesn't know where to start.

---

**Day 1: Priya connects her accounts**

She links her Fidelity 401(k), Schwab brokerage, Chase checking, and uploads her last tax return. Meridian builds her profile: income sources, account balances, holdings, tax situation, vesting schedule.

She sets two goals: retire at 55, and fund her daughter's college (starting in 14 years).

---

**First open: the Overview dashboard**

Meridian shows two progress bars:
- **Retire at 55**: On track at current savings rate, but assumes 7% return and $12k/month spending. 3 findings flagged.
- **College fund**: Underfunded. At current 529 contribution rate, projected to cover 60% of a 4-year private university.

She taps into **Tax** because she's been worried about it.

---

**Tax dashboard**

Meridian shows her estimated current-year tax liability: $94,000 federal. It surfaces four findings:

1. **RSU vesting tax opportunity**: $180k of RSUs vest in November. If she defers some of the sale to January, she shifts $45k of income to next tax year — estimated savings: $14,000.
2. **Asset location mismatch**: Her bond fund ($22k) is in her taxable account. Moving it to her 401(k) and replacing it with equities in taxable would reduce annual tax drag by ~$900.
3. **Backdoor Roth unused**: She and her husband are above the direct Roth IRA income limit but haven't done a backdoor Roth this year. $13,000 of tax-free growth space unclaimed.
4. **No HSA**: Her employer offers an HSA-eligible plan. Switching would save ~$2,200/year in payroll taxes and create a tax-advantaged medical account.

She asks in chat: "If I do all four of these, what's the total impact?" The agent responds: estimated $17,100 in tax savings this year, plus $900/year ongoing, plus the compounding value of the Roth space.

---

**She creates a Brief: "Retire at 55"**

She taps New Brief and types her goal. The agent runs a full retirement analysis:

**Headline:** At your current trajectory, you can retire at 55 with approximately $9,400/month in today's dollars, assuming 7% returns, 3% inflation, and $144k/year in retirement spending.

**The brief walks through:**
- Current assets and projected growth curve
- RSU vesting schedule contribution
- Social Security at 62 vs. 67
- Sequence-of-returns risk in the first 5 years of retirement
- Whether the college goal conflicts with the retirement goal (it does, slightly — Meridian flags it)

**She pulls a lever:** changes retirement spending to $180k/year. The headline updates: retire at 57, not 55. She asks "what's the single biggest lever to get back to 55?" The agent responds: increasing savings rate by 4% ($11k/year) closes most of the gap.

---

**Three months later**

Priya opens the app. The Tax dashboard now shows the RSU deferral opportunity has passed (she didn't act on it in time — the agent flags this as a missed opportunity with estimated cost). Her investment allocation has drifted 4% toward equities after a strong quarter — Meridian flags a rebalancing opportunity. Her 529 contribution increased last month; the college goal bar moved from 60% to 64% projected coverage.

She doesn't need to schedule a meeting with anyone. She opens the app, sees what changed, and knows what to do next.

## Why now

- AI models are now capable enough to synthesize complex, multi-domain financial data and produce specific, accurate analysis — not just generic summaries.
- Account aggregation APIs (Plaid, MX) have made connecting financial accounts fast and reliable.
- The financial advisory industry has a trust problem and an access problem. Good advice is expensive and infrequent. People know it.
- High-income earners are increasingly comfortable with AI tools in their professional lives and ready to trust them with financial analysis (if not execution).

---

## What Meridian is not

- It is not a robo-advisor. It does not manage money.
- It is not a financial advisor. It does not give legally binding advice.
- It is not a budgeting app. It is not primarily for tracking spending.
- It does not sell financial products or earn commissions.

Meridian is an analysis and planning layer that sits on top of wherever your money actually lives.
